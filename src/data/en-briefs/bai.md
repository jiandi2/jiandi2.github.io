Yan Bai is a professor of economics at the University of Rochester, jointly affiliated with the Chinese University of Hong Kong, and a co-editor of the *Journal of International Economics*. She is among the leading researchers of quantitative sovereign-default models. The interview almost refuses to begin there.

She did not want to go abroad. The China Center for Economic Research at Peking University sent her anyway — teachers who landed from trans-Pacific flights and went straight to the workshop. Minnesota meant two years of mathematics; then a seat beside Edward Prescott. The craft chapter is more revealing still: a classroom assignment from Patrick Kehoe on the Obstfeld–Rogoff puzzles, the Feldstein–Horioka problem, an *Econometrica* paper with Jing Zhang, and an editor who said, round after round, that the next round would be the last.

Her judgments are specific. Trade frictions — tariffs, sanctions — are still largely outside sovereign-default models, and should not be. She does not hand students topics; she would rather they struggle, even if they end up teaching her a literature she does not yet know. And she resists the reflex of sorting talent by gender before the work has spoken: to divide people first into men and women, she says, may itself be a form of prejudice.

The conversation is about research as a craft that can be taught, and as a temperament that cannot be. Along the way there is a warning from Andrew Atkeson not to do theory, and an editor who threatened, round after round, to reject the paper next time. Anyone who has watched a homework problem become a paper, or wondered what remains of a researcher once computation is cheap, will recognize the stakes.
