Jianjun Miao is Qingshan Chair Professor and dean of the School of Economics at Zhejiang University, after a long career as a tenured professor at Boston University. He is known for work on heterogeneous-agent models, asset bubbles, and decision-making under uncertainty. The interview is less a tour of papers than a reconstruction of a theoretical temperament.

Rochester left an imprint: general equilibrium as a way of seeing, not a technique. Miao’s adviser was Larry Epstein; the department still carried Lionel McKenzie’s insistence on microfoundations. The hard turn, Miao says, was not learning more mathematics — he had trained in math at the University of Science and Technology of China — but learning to stop solving problems and start telling stories that still had to be true.

He is frank about leaving Boston University to build a research community in China, including the China International Conference in Macroeconomics. The return is not narrated as a victory lap. It is a bet that a field has to be grown, not merely visited. Theory, in this telling, is not a luxury imported from elsewhere. It is a way of asking whether a Chinese fact is a Chinese fact, or a general one wearing local clothes.

The conversation matters beyond China studies because it is about what theory is for. A model that cannot be explained in words has not yet become economics. A community that cannot sustain such models will import conclusions and never produce them. Miao’s insistence on intuition is therefore not anti-mathematical. It is a standard for when the mathematics has finally earned the right to be believed.
