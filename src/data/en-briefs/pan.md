Jun Pan is a chair professor at the Shanghai Advanced Institute of Finance at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. Before returning to her alma mater in 2019, she spent nineteen years at MIT Sloan. With Darrell Duffie and Kenneth Singleton she wrote “Transform Analysis and Asset Pricing for Affine Jump-Diffusions,” a paper that became infrastructure for option pricing, credit risk, and term-structure models, and later received the Stephen A. Ross Prize. She now edits the *Review of Finance*.

The interview is not a victory lap. Pan’s path — physics at Shanghai Jiao Tong, a physics Ph.D. at NYU, finance at Stanford GSB — was a sequence of choices rather than a plan. She is dry about the difference. Planning, in her telling, is often a story one tells afterward. What matters is whether you can recognize a door, and walk through it without demanding that the corridor already be labeled.

Two later turns give the conversation its edge. One is editorial: what a top finance journal is actually judging, once the mathematics is no longer the scarce resource. The other is political economy in the narrow sense — her work with Zhe Geng on the SOE premium in China’s credit market, and the moment when government stopped being a background condition and became an object of asset pricing. The 2019 return to Shanghai Jiao Tong belongs with those turns. It was a choice, she suggests, not the execution of an old plan.

Scholars who have never written a China paper will still recognize the problem. Markets do not live outside the state. A pricing kernel that cannot see government is not more scientific. It is less complete. The same is true of a career: the interesting path is rarely the one that was specified in advance.
