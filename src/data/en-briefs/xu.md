Daniel Yi Xu is the David Rubenstein Distinguished Professor of Economics at Duke University, an NBER research associate, and co-editor of the *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics*. His paper “How Costly Are Markups?” received the 2025 Robert E. Lucas Jr. Prize. The interview is about how an empiricist is made, and what the making costs.

He did not arrive as a theorist in waiting. At Shanghai University of Finance and Economics he studied international finance, which in practice meant English and accounting. The Ph.D. was a pragmatic turn: a program that paid tuition. The accounting, it turned out, was the lasting gift — firm reports, statistical-bureau data, and tax records speak the same language. Later there is a story about buying a decade of Taiwanese plant-level data, because the question was true and the data were not free.

Xu’s China work is unapologetically second-best. Industrial policy, R&D tax incentives, markups: the point is not to scold a government or to bless it, but to measure the cost of a distortion in a world that will not wait for the first best. That is a general empirical ethic, not a regional specialty. The Lucas Prize paper asks, in that spirit, how costly markups actually are — a question that does not require a China application to matter, and does not become less general when it has one.

For a scholar deciding whether this series is serious, the signal is the texture. The conversation remembers invoices, not slogans. It treats data as something one has to obtain, clean, and defend — which is how empirical economics actually happens. The brief cannot reproduce that labor. It can only point to it, and send the reader to the Chinese record.
